ICEBREAKER AND THE GRID

Many ice batteries working as one

Thermal virtual power plants (VPPs) pre-charge buildings at night, then use stored cooling across the fleet when the grid is under heat-driven strain. They shave hot-hour energy peaks.

Why it matters
Many buildings peak at the same time

On the hottest afternoons, cooling load stacks across a feeder, a campus, or a utility territory.

What the fleet does
Trim compressor load without losing comfort

Stored cooling lets the utility call for less compressor power while buildings stay operational.

Why Icebreaker fits
Cooling load is predictable and recurring

That makes pre-charge and dispatch easier to explain than a broader DER story.

Pre-Charge At Night. Dispatch On Peak Days.
Night Fleet pre-charges

Buildings fill thermal storage before the critical afternoon window.

Peak event Compressors throttle down

Stored cooling covers part of the load when the feeder or system is stressed.

Grid result Lower coincident peak

The fleet behaves like a targeted flexible demand resource for heat-wave conditions.

What the utility is buying
Hot-afternoon peak relief from commercial cooling load
